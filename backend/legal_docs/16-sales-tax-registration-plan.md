# Sales-Tax Registration Plan (Draft)

## 1. Objective
Bring Birthright into compliance with state sales-tax collection
obligations arising from remote sales of tangible personal property.

## 2. Nexus Analysis
Post-Wayfair (South Dakota v. Wayfair, 2018) states may impose economic
nexus once a remote seller exceeds a revenue or transaction threshold.
Common thresholds: $100,000 in sales OR 200 transactions in the prior
calendar year.

**Action items for counsel/accountant**:
1. Review the last 12 months of Stripe sales data (available at
   `/admin/reports`) grouped by ship-to state.
2. Identify states in which either the revenue or the transaction
   threshold has been met.
3. Register for a sales-tax permit in each such state.
4. Configure Stripe Tax (or equivalent) to collect and remit going
   forward.

## 3. Product Taxability
- Apparel, prints, mugs, bags, journals, patches: taxable in most states.
- Digital workshops: taxability varies (some states tax digital
  educational products, some exempt when accompanied by live instruction).
- Nonprofit exemption: **not automatic**. Even if 501(c)(3), most states
  require the buyer (not the seller) to hold the exemption to purchase
  tax-free.

## 4. Marketplace Facilitator Considerations
For any items fulfilled via Printful or Lulu, confirm whether they act as
"marketplace facilitator" for tax purposes. If yes, we may not need to
register in states where they already remit. Document this in a
compliance memo.

## 5. Filing Cadence
Monthly, quarterly, or annually depending on state and volume.
